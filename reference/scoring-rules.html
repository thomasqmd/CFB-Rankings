Scoring rules: quick reference

Compressed from the course. Each card names the lesson it came from.

Brier score

BS = (p − o)²

  • p = forecast probability of a yes/no event, o = 1 if it happened, 0 if not. Average over games.
  • Anchors: 0 perfect · 0.25 always 50% · 1 certain and wrong.
  • Unitless (probability scale). Proper: honest probabilities score best on average.
  • Your backtest: 0.166 (19,177 games).

Lesson 2

CRPS: three equivalent forms

∫ ( F(t) − 1{y ≤ t} )² dt

  • By threshold: the Brier score of "margin ≤ t?", integrated over every t.
  • By quantile: twice the average pinball loss over probability levels. This is crps_quantiles(), R/03_rankings.R:288.
  • By draws: E|X − y| − ½ E|X − X′|.
    mean(abs(x - y)) - 0.5 * mean(abs(x - sample(x)))
  • Units: points. Lower is better. Never negative.

Lesson 2

How they connect

  • Brier = the CRPS slice at t = 0. F(0) = 1 − p, so (F(0) − 1{y ≤ 0})² = (p − o)².
  • MAE = CRPS with no spread. A point forecast m has CRPS |m − y|.
  • So MAE and CRPS share a ruler. Your backtest: MAE 12.9 → CRPS 9.2.
  • Spread costs when the centre is right (≈ 0.23σ for a Normal; 3.5 pts at σ = 15) and pays when it's wrong.

Lesson 2

Which score answers which question

  • Who wins? (win probability, playoff odds) → Brier score.
  • By how much? (the margin distribution) → CRPS.
  • Disagreement is informative. High Brier with ordinary CRPS: the forecast was on the wrong side of zero but not far off (The Game 2024, 0.675 / 10.4). Low Brier with huge CRPS: right winner, wrong margin (Orange Bowl 2023, 0.095 / 44.3).

Lesson 2

Closed form, Normal forecast

σ [ z(2Φ(z) − 1) + 2φ(z) − 1/√π ],  z = (y − μ)/σ

  • Centred (z = 0): σ(2φ(0) − 1/√π) ≈ 0.234σ.
  • Student-t version in your code: crps_t(), R/04_validation.R:120.

Lesson 2

Proper scoring rule

A score is proper if your best expected score comes from forecasting what you actually believe, and strictly proper if that is the only way to get it. Brier and CRPS are both proper. Absolute error on a probability, |p − o|, is not: it rewards rounding 70% up to 100%.

Lesson 2 · Gneiting & Raftery (2007)

Credible sets (Lesson 1)

  • Equal-tailed (ETI): quantiles (1 − L)/2, (1 + L)/2. Pipeline default (R/03_rankings.R:126). Invariant to monotone transforms.
  • HDI: shortest window of sorted draws holding L. Best for skewed. Not transform-invariant.
  • Highest-density set: most probable values until L. Ranks, multimodal. Can split.
  • Normal approx.: mean ± z·sd. Symmetric, unbounded only.
  • Bounded quantities (ranks near 1, probabilities near 1): ETI over-covers. ND 2025: ETI 1–9 holds 92%; HDI set 1–6 holds 81%.
  • Tail endpoints carry the most Monte Carlo error: posterior::mcse_quantile().
  • R: ggdist::qi(), hdci(), hdi().

Log score (Lesson 3)

LogS = −log f(y)

  • Nats, lower better, strictly proper, local (only the density at y).
  • Δ of 1 nat = e ≈ 2.7× density ratio.
  • elpd = Σ log f(y): same thing, sign flipped (higher better). Log loss = binary version on win prob.
  • Tail-sensitive: Normal grows like z²/2; CRPS grows linearly. Family choice → log score has the stronger opinion.
  • From draws: scoringRules::logs_sample(y, dat) (kernel density; fragile in far tails).
  • Your backtest: mean ≈ 4.22 nats; Orange Bowl ≈ 11.

Interval score (Lesson 4)

(u − l) + (2/α)(l − y)₊ + (2/α)(y − u)₊

  • α = 1 − level. 80% → each point of miss costs 10.
  • Proper. = (pinballlo + pinballhi)/α. Average pinball over all levels → CRPS.
  • Coverage alone is not a score (ignores width).
  • Your model: best width multiplier 1.00× (50%), 1.05× (80%, 95%). Coverage 49.8 / 79.2 / 94.5%.

Lesson 4 · FPP3 §5.9

PIT & calibration (Lesson 5)

  • PIT u = F(y). Ideal forecasts → uniform.
  • U = too narrow · hump = too wide · slope = biased (many high u → centre too low).
  • Integer outcomes with a step forecast: randomised PIT between F(y − 1) and F(y).
  • Maximise sharpness subject to calibration. Climatology is calibrated and useless (CRPS 11.75 vs 9.14).

Comparing models (Lesson 6)

  • Same games, per-game differences di = SB − SA.
  • SE clustered by season-week (dm(), R/04_validation.R:247); naive SEs up to 2.6× too small.
  • Real (z) ≠ matters (size). Set a practical threshold first.
  • Skill = 1 − Smodel/Sref: +22% vs climatology, −3.7% vs the line.
